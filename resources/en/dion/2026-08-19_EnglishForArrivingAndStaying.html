    CLEARED

    American life has its own vocabulary, and no one explains it to you. Tap any word to hear it.

    Paperwork

    Social Security number
    Nine digits. You'll need it for a job, a bank account, a phone plan.
    หมายเลขประกันสังคม — ใช้ทำงาน เปิดบัญชี สมัครเบอร์โทร
    Green card
    Permanent resident card. It isn't green anymore.
    บัตรผู้มีถิ่นที่อยู่ถาวร
    Fill out a form
    Not "fill in the form" — Americans say fill out.
    กรอกแบบฟอร์ม (อเมริกันใช้ fill out)
    ZIP code
    The five-number postcode. Asked for constantly.
    รหัสไปรษณีย์

    Money

    Credit score
    A number between 300 and 850. It decides your rent, your car, your phone.
    คะแนนเครดิต มีผลกับการเช่าบ้าน ผ่อนรถ
    Utilities
    Electricity, gas, water, internet — the monthly bills.
    ค่าน้ำ ค่าไฟ ค่าแก๊ส ค่าเน็ต
    Security deposit
    Money you pay the landlord and hope to get back.
    เงินมัดจำที่จ่ายให้เจ้าของบ้าน
    Direct deposit
    Your pay goes straight into your bank account.
    เงินเดือนโอนเข้าบัญชีโดยตรง

    Health

    Co-pay
    The fixed amount you pay at each visit, even with insurance.
    ส่วนที่ต้องจ่ายเองทุกครั้งที่ไปหาหมอ แม้มีประกัน
    Deductible
    What you pay yourself before insurance starts paying.
    ยอดที่ต้องจ่ายเองก่อน ประกันถึงจะเริ่มจ่าย
    Prescription
    The doctor's order for medicine. Refill = get more of it.
    ใบสั่งยา / refill คือรับยาต่อ
    Make an appointment
    Almost nothing here is walk-in. You book first.
    นัดหมายล่วงหน้า ที่นี่แทบไม่มี walk-in
    Teacher notes
    • Six minutes, twelve items — don't teach all of them. Ask "which three are new?" and drill those.
    • Check stress: SO-cial SE-curity, de-DUC-ti-ble, pre-SCRIP-tion, u-TIL-ities.
    • Ties to her interest in business & finance — credit score is a good hook for a future lesson.

    Seven lines that buy you time

    • Sorry, could you say that again a little slower?
      ขอโทษค่ะ พูดอีกครั้งช้าๆ ได้ไหมคะ
    • I'm not sure I follow.
      ไม่ค่อยแน่ใจว่าเข้าใจถูกไหม
    • I'm still getting used to it.
      ยังปรับตัวอยู่ค่ะ
    • How do you say this in English?
      อันนี้ภาษาอังกฤษเรียกว่าอะไร
    • That works for me.
      โอเคเลย สะดวกค่ะ
    • Let me think about it and get back to you.
      ขอคิดดูก่อน แล้วจะติดต่อกลับ
    • I've heard so much about you.
      ได้ยินเรื่องคุณมาเยอะเลยค่ะ (เจอครอบครัวสามีครั้งแรก)

    Five sounds that Thai doesn't have

    Tap each word. Say them back. Most Thai speakers lose the ending, not the beginning.

    แตะฟังแล้วพูดตาม คนไทยส่วนใหญ่ตกที่เสียงท้ายคำ ไม่ใช่เสียงต้นคำ

    wet / vet
    rice / rise
    tree / three
    low / row
    cash / cast

    And the one that changes meaning: a card / two cards, one month / six months.

    Teacher notes
    • Thai has no final /s/, /l/, /v/, /θ/ and no final consonant clusters — so plurals, third person -s, and past -ed disappear. This is usually the single biggest gap for an otherwise strong B1.
    • Don't drill all five. Pick the two that actually showed up in stage 01.
    • Praise specifically: name the sound she got right.

    CLEARED

    Five scenes, one for each thing you're into. Choose one and we'll play it out — I'll take the other part.

    เลือกหนึ่งสถานการณ์ แล้วเราจะเล่นบทบาทสมมติกัน ครูรับอีกบทเอง

    Sunday dinner at your in-laws' house. Someone made a casserole. Someone asks you what Thai food is "really" like. You want to be warm, not just polite.

    • This is delicious — what's in it?
    • I'm so full, but maybe just a little more.
    • It's a bit like that, but we use more lime and chili.
    • Next time, let me cook for you.
    1. What would you cook to introduce Thai food to Americans?
    2. What's one American dish you still don't understand?
    3. How do you say no to a third helping without offending anyone?

    At the bank, opening your first account. The teller is fast and friendly. You need to understand fees, and you want to know how to start building credit.

    • Is there a monthly maintenance fee?
    • What's the minimum balance to avoid it?
    • I'm new to the country, so I don't have a credit history yet.
    • Could you write that down for me?
    1. What surprised you most about how money works here?
    2. How is banking different in Thailand?
    3. What would you want to be able to do in English at a bank by December?

    Your neighbour catches you at the mailbox. "So what are you watching these days?" You have eight seconds to have an opinion.

    • I'm halfway through the second season.
    • No spoilers!
    • It starts slow, but it's worth it.
    • I watch with subtitles — honestly, it helps a lot.
    1. Tell me the plot of something you loved, in five sentences.
    2. Do you watch with English or Thai subtitles? Why?
    3. Which show do Americans keep telling you to watch?

    Returning a foundation that's the wrong shade. You opened it. You have the receipt. The assistant says "hmm."

    • I'd like to return this — it's the wrong shade for me.
    • Can I exchange it instead?
    • Could you match it to my skin tone?
    • Do you have this in a lighter or warmer tone?
    1. What's harder to buy here than in Thailand?
    2. Describe your skincare routine — I'll only interrupt for pronunciation.
    3. Has a shop assistant ever spoken too fast for you? What did you do?

    First visit to a new doctor. New forms, new vocabulary, and a nurse asking about your family history.

    • I've had this for about two weeks.
    • It comes and goes.
    • I'm allergic to…
    • Sorry — what does that word mean?
    1. Explain a symptom to me without using the medical word for it.
    2. What's one science topic you'd read about in English for fun?
    3. What would you want to understand better before your next appointment?
    Teacher notes
    • This is the diagnostic. Let her talk 70% of these seven minutes; take notes instead of correcting.
    • Track three things only: past/perfect tense accuracy, final consonants, and how she repairs a breakdown.
    • Escalate once — throw in one fast, idiomatic line and see whether she asks for repetition. That's a skill, not a failure.
